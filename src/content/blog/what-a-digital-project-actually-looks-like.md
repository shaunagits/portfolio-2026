Most clients have hired a web professional before and still are not sure what the process is supposed to look like. Projects run long, feedback rounds multiply, and the final product arrives later and differently than expected. That is usually a process problem, not a talent problem.

Here is how I run a project from first conversation to launch.

---

## Discovery

Every project starts with a conversation. I ask about your goals, your audience, your existing setup, and what success looks like. I am not filling out a template. I am trying to understand your business well enough to make real decisions on your behalf.

After that conversation I put together a proposal that defines the scope, the timeline, and the investment. When you sign off and pay the deposit, the project starts.

---

## Strategy and Planning

Before anything gets designed or built, we align on the structure. What pages does the site need? What does each page need to accomplish? What is the user supposed to do and feel at each step?

This work is less visible than design but more important. Decisions made here shape everything that follows. Skipping it is how projects end up needing to be rebuilt.

---

## Design

I work in high-fidelity mockups, which means what you are reviewing looks like a real website, not a rough sketch. You can see the typography, the colors, the layout, and the content in place.

You get a defined number of revision rounds. Feedback is most useful when it is consolidated. One round of clear, specific notes moves faster than five rounds of small changes from multiple people.

---

## Development

Once design is approved, I build it. Depending on the project, this might mean building in a CMS, writing custom code, setting up integrations, or some combination of all three.

I test across devices and browsers before anything goes to you for review.

---

## Review and Revisions

You get the site in a staging environment before anything goes live. This is your opportunity to click through everything, test any forms or functionality, and flag anything that needs adjustment.

This is also the point where having your content finalized matters. A site that launches with placeholder copy or missing images is not a finished site.

---

## Launch

When everything is approved, we launch. I handle the technical side of moving the site live, connecting the domain, and making sure everything is working correctly.

Launch is not the end of the relationship. Questions come up. Small things need adjusting. I am reachable after a project closes.

---

## After Launch

Most of my clients come back for ongoing work. A second phase of the site, a new feature, an annual update. A website is not a one-time project for a growing business. It is something that should evolve as you do.
